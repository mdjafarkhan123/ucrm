# P2B clinical records — source review

**Checked 2026-10-06.** Research for the US-first, single-location-first Medspa & Clinical Wellness plan. Sources below are first-party documentation; “proposed” is a product choice, not a claim that Boulevard has it. Jafar agreed the four decisions below on 2026-10-06; the resulting behavior is in [the plan](../boulevard-product-behavior-contract.md#clinical-records). The [existing inventory](boulevard-clinical-engagement-inventory-2026-10-05.md) provides the capability map.

## Confirmed behavior and implications

| Area | Confirmed source behavior | P2B implication / proposed behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake and consent | Boulevard has reusable, service/location-triggered **client forms** (medical history, waivers, photo consent) and **staff-only charts** (SOAP/treatment notes). Forms can be once-only, every appointment, or expire; delivery can be with confirmation/reminders or at check-in. Submitted forms/charts lock. A form can be marked completed offline, and a submitted form can be archived and replaced. [Builder](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/6989798-forms-and-charts-building-forms-and-charts); [submission actions](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941366-forms-submitting-printing-saving-sending-and-deleting). Jane retains each repeated intake submission rather than overwriting it. [Jane intake](https://jane.app/guide/intake-forms). | **Proposed:** retain each signed response with template/version, subject, signer, signature time, and appointment/service context. Make service-specific treatment consent distinct from general history updates and optional photo publicity authorization. A form’s expiration or completion status alone cannot prove clinical suitability. |
| Consent as a clinical decision | The AMA describes informed consent as a patient–clinician communication covering intervention, risks/benefits, alternatives and forgoing treatment, then documenting the patient/surrogate decision in the record; a written consent goes into that record. [AMA ethics opinion](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/informed-consent). HHS says uses/disclosures of protected health information for marketing generally require signed authorization and, with limited exceptions, treatment cannot be conditioned on it. [HHS health-information guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/hipaa-ftc-act/index.html). | **Proposed:** the clinic owns the consent wording and clinician conversation. Store treatment consent and optional before/after publicity authorization as separate decisions, with revocation/status handling for publicity. Do not imply a signature substitutes for the clinician’s suitability assessment. |
| Parent/guardian and child | Boulevard’s public material does not document a family/guardian form flow (P2A inventory). Jane lets a relationship with explicit permission complete a form **on the family member’s profile** and see only forms that relationship submitted, not other forms; Jane’s related person cannot view the patient’s shared chart through their own account. [Jane family forms](https://jane.app/guide/how-do-family-members-fill-out-intake-forms-online); [Jane relationships](https://jane.app/guide/client-relationships). | **Proposed:** each child has their own record; capture who supplied answers, who signed, and in what capacity. Do **not** inherit Jane’s blanket chart-access limit as the clinical access rule. See HHS below. |
| Parental medical-record access | HHS OCR’s **December 2025** letter says a parent who is a minor’s personal representative generally has a right to the child’s medical records and electronic access; default portal settings cannot improperly deny it. Exceptions may be **service/record-specific**: minor-consented care where law allows, court-directed care, agreed confidential care, applicable state-law limits, or individualized professional judgment about abuse/endangerment. [OCR letter](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr-letter-hipaa-privacy-rule-and-parental-access-to-minor-childrens-medical-records.pdf); [HHS FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/can-i-access-medical-record-if-i-have-power-of-attorney/index.html). An adult’s effective health-care power of attorney may separately make another person a personal representative. [HHS POA FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/does-having-health-care-power-attorney-allow-access-patients-medical-mental-health-records-under-hipaa/index.html). | **P2A correction recorded:** minor-based parent access ends at the applicable adult age, but an adult may grant separate legally effective representative authority. Before adulthood, records cannot all be hidden behind Jane-style portal behavior; access must follow the representative’s legal authority and the particular record’s restrictions. Portal display is a delivery method, not the source of the right. Product needs an authenticated proxy route and a staff path for legally required record access when portal settings cannot express an exception. State-specific legal review remains necessary before launch. |
| Charting, photos, corrections | Boulevard lets staff capture photos in forms/charts, annotate chart diagrams/photos with pins/shapes, and compare images in a gallery. Its camera capture does not save a second device copy; Duo check-in cannot upload photos. [Upload](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8461332-forms-and-charts-photo-upload); [markup](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8496145-forms-and-charts-photo-markup); [gallery](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8944888-photo-gallery). Copy-previous excludes date/signature but **does duplicate previous images under today’s date**. [Copy previous](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/11157720-forms-and-charts-copy-previous-chart). Boulevard can add field notes after submission, but editing a note replaces its former author/time and deleting it is permanent. [Form notes](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/6326021-forms-add-notes-to-forms). | **Proposed:** chart per treated person and visit; capture original image, annotation and capture context separately. Copy prior text only as an explicit draft aid; never silently recast old photos as new. Preserve signed records and use attributed, time-stamped amendments rather than replacing the original. Clinical photos stay separate from publicity permission. |
| Review/sign-off | Boulevard can route a submitted form/chart to “Needs Review,” then reviewer approval/signature to “Completed”; permissions also allow bulk sign-off or “Completed without review.” [Boulevard sign-off](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8681202-sign-off-on-completed-forms-and-charts). Aesthetic Record has provider-signed charts sent to a medical director who can ask for more information. [Aesthetic Record MD review](https://learn.aestheticrecord.com/en/articles/9251912-sign-charts-and-send-to-md-provider-md-room-guide). | **Proposed:** separate author submission, actual reviewer approval, and an authorized “review not required” disposition in status/history. A bulk action is not evidence of review. Clinical sign-off generally follows documentation; it should not silently count as pre-treatment clearance. Which services/roles require review remains a clinic-policy question. |
| Prerequisite clearance | Zenoti ties a main service to a completed consultation/patch test with validity and possible equivalent prior service; booking can enforce the prerequisite. [Zenoti setup](https://help.zenoti.com/en/consumer-experience/webstore/admin-tasks-for-booking-appointments.html). Aesthetic Record shows a service-level good-faith-exam status/expiry on appointment cards and procedures; its red status is a visible warning, not proof that a clinician passed the person. [Aesthetic Record GFE](https://learn.aestheticrecord.com/en/articles/14891707-understanding-and-managing-gfe-status-indicators). | **Proposed:** record a prerequisite **outcome** (cleared / not cleared / needs follow-up), deciding clinician, completion and expiry dates, applicable services, and evidence visit/chart. P2A’s “completed, and where required passed” should only unlock eligible booking when that outcome is current. Cancellation, reversal or expiry re-flags existing treatment bookings. Staff booking override and clinical clearance should be different permissions/events. Whether an exam permits same-day treatment and how long it lasts are clinic/state policy, not a universal default. |
| Medication and ePrescribe | Boulevard’s ScriptSure integration requires eligible clinical plan, US patient demographics, prescriber NPI/license and identity proofing; assistants may queue prescriptions for prescriber approval. Controlled-substance setup adds DEA and a second admin/EPCS authentication. [Getting started](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/13773728-getting-started-with-eprescribe); [patient prescriptions](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/14152011-manage-patient-prescriptions-in-eprescribe). Form-connected allergy/medication **notes do not automatically become ScriptSure safety-check data**; users must reconcile them there. [Allergy/medication workflow](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/14489548-eprescribe-tracking-client-medication-and-allergies). ScriptSure is also a separate location practice and Boulevard is its sole access path. [Boulevard FAQ](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/14556542-eprescribe-faqs). | **Proposed:** plan intake medication/allergy collection and clinician reconciliation as separate steps. Do not label free-text intake as an interaction check. An integrated eRx launch requires a qualified partner, credentialing, EPCS workflows and outage path; otherwise leave prescribing outside the initial product promise, with a later integration decision. |

## Decisions agreed by Jafar 2026-10-06

1. **Parent portal scope:** Verified parent/guardian access to legally accessible child records, with record-level exceptions and a staff-managed legal access route. Jane-style “only forms I filled out” is a poor universal fit given HHS guidance. Release timing remains P3's decision.
2. **Clinical gate strength:** Required clearance is an explicit clinician outcome linked to the qualifying visit. Service booking and treatment start are distinct checks; a booking override never grants clinical clearance.
3. **Prescribing scope:** No native prescription creation without an established eRx integration, prescriber credentialing, required controlled-substance controls, and an outage path. Release timing remains P3's decision.
4. **Review policy:** Per-template sign-off configuration, named reviewer and explicit “review not required” status. Checkout can finish with review still pending and visible.

**Evidence limits:** Vendor help pages prove vendor UI behavior, not legal sufficiency. HHS explains federal privacy rules and defers several minor-consent/access questions to state law. No universal medspa-specific consent text, exam validity period, or parent-access age rule was established here. No application work or release assignment is implied.
