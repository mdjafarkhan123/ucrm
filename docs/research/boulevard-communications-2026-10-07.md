# Boulevard communications — P2D research

**Researched:** 2026-10-07. **Status:** First-party public-source evidence and proposals for planning; no release assignment or product approval. Boulevard behavior is identified as **Evidence**. Our recommended rules are identified as **Proposal**. Vendor documentation does not establish legal compliance or suitability for every clinic.

## Appointment notices and confirmation

- **Evidence — Boulevard:** A location configures booking confirmation, reminders, receipts, rating requests, and after-hours replies in Client Notifications. Booking confirmation sends at booking; reminder timing can be set 1–5 days before; a basic reminder can skip confirmed clients; same-day texts reach confirmed and unconfirmed clients. Text notices require a dedicated registered number and count toward usage. [Client Notifications](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8447955-client-notifications); [Booking Confirmation](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8447995-appointment-booking-confirmations).
- **Evidence — Boulevard:** The initial text can ask an unconfirmed client to reply “yes.” The message may include an appointment-management link, outstanding-form link, and cancellation policy; extra text cannot be marketing content. [Booking confirmation texts FAQ](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8447942-booking-confirmation-texts-faq); [Appointment Booking Confirmations](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8447995-appointment-booking-confirmations).
- **Evidence — Boulevard flaw:** Boulevard says any affirmation word in a reply, including a message saying the client wishes to cancel, can trigger appointment confirmation. This is explicit in its cancellation guide, not merely an inference. [Canceling Appointments](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941385-canceling-appointments).
- **Proposal:** Let each business choose limited reminder timing and channel. Confirmation changes the appointment state only through an authenticated appointment action or an unambiguous, exact reply to the current appointment prompt (for example, trimmed standalone `YES`, tied to one pending appointment); a reply containing any other words remains a staff message. A text such as “yes, please cancel” must never confirm. Cancellation and rescheduling use the secure appointment link or staff, and confirmations/opt-outs must be reflected promptly in the schedule and send queue. This is our safeguard, not documented Boulevard behavior.
- **Proposal — delivery:** Staff can see pending, sent, failed, replied and confirmed states, and can follow up on failed delivery. This extends Boulevard's visible SMS failure indicator and shared conversation history. [Messages](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941436-messages); [Message Delivery Errors](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/9341002-message-delivery-errors).

## Shared inbox and handoff

- **Evidence — Boulevard:** One location number carries two-way staff texts, notification texts and marketing texts into a shared client history. Conversations can be open/closed or read/unread, carry staff-only notes, and show failed sends. Away and after-hours responses are separate; after-hours replies are capped at one per client per 12 hours. Viewing, sending, changing away status, and changing notification settings have distinct permissions. [Messages](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941436-messages); [Client Notifications](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8447955-client-notifications).
- **Proposal:** Replies to automated appointment or campaign texts land in a human-owned queue with a clear owner, unread state, and last-response time. Automated messages do not silently close an active client request. Staff can see whether a message is a reply to a reminder, marketing send or ordinary conversation. Clinical concern, adverse reaction, medication question, or urgent wording moves to a trained human route; the inbox does not present an automated answer as medical advice. This handoff design is ours; the source documents only Boulevard's common inbox and conversation states.

## Waitlist alert

- **Evidence — Boulevard:** Joining the waitlist sends the client a confirmation email and alerts staff on the Dashboard about **new entries**. Staff review a requested service, provider, time and notes and manually book or remove the entry. Its public page does not document an automatic alert when a matching slot later opens. [Waitlist](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941433-waitlist).
- **Evidence — Boulevard integration:** REACH.ai is listed as a separate partner integration that scans the schedule for last-minute openings and sends eligible clients booking links according to consent. That is not evidence of native Boulevard waitlist-offer behavior. [Boulevard Integrations](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/7880786-boulevard-integrations).
- **Proposal:** When a cancellation, move or published shift creates a time matching an existing waitlist request, put a staff alert in the calendar/front-desk work queue, with the matched requests and booking action. Staff pick and contact/book the client; do not promise the slot to multiple people or auto-text a clinical treatment offer. Keep a visible new-entry alert as well. This fulfills the P2A agreement while leaving automatic client offers for separate consideration.

## Marketing consent and targeting

- **Evidence — Boulevard:** Boulevard distinguishes appointment/operational communication from promotional email and text. Its portal exposes channel preferences and unsubscribe controls; `NO PROMOS` stops promotional texts only, while `STOP`/`UNSUBSCRIBE` stops all texts from the business number. Staff may set email marketing preferences in limited circumstances; a client must take their own action to opt into text marketing. Existing opt-outs persist across later booking. [Marketing Consent FAQ](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/6574083-marketing-consent-faq); [Marketing Opt-In Preferences](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/6592791-marketing-opt-in-preferences).
- **Evidence — consent conflict:** Boulevard's self-booking notice says booking also opts the client into promotional messages. Twilio's current US verification guidance says a business may not make SMS marketing opt-in a condition of using its service and calls for a separate unchecked choice and express written marketing consent. This establishes a provider-policy reason **not to copy** Boulevard's combined booking notice; it does not decide every legal question about email or healthcare marketing. [Boulevard Marketing Consent FAQ](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/6574083-marketing-consent-faq); [Twilio verification error 30830](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30830).
- **Proposal:** Booking and care messages have their own channel preferences and permitted purpose. Promotional email and promotional text have separate opt-in status, source, timestamp and withdrawal history. Booking can finish when promotional boxes are left unchecked. A prior `STOP` blocks further texts until a valid re-opt-in; marketing suppression cannot be bypassed by a saved audience, imported list, AI or staff send. Do not interpret a booking, phone number, past purchase, or parent link as marketing consent. Confirm final wording and state-specific healthcare marketing rules before launch. [Twilio verification error 30830](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/30830); [Boulevard Marketing Consent Import](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/9892885-marketing-consent-import).
- **Evidence — Boulevard targeting:** One-time text/email blasts use saved audiences with activity, service, product, membership and marketing-status filters. A campaign may exclude a saved audience and people recently marketed to. The five automated campaigns are preset types with adjustable thresholds and channel; the docs do not prove a general-purpose automation builder. Replies to text blasts enter Messages; replies to email blasts go to the location's email address. [Clients & Audiences](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/9077071-clients-audiences); [Text Blasts FAQ](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/9391745-text-blasts-faq); [Email Blasts Campaign Setup](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/10088521-email-blasts-campaign-setup); [Automated Campaigns Overview](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/10068551-automated-campaigns-overview-setup).
- **Proposal:** A preview shows the actual eligible count after consent, exclusions, recent-send limits and access scope; recheck eligibility at send time. Keep a record of who launched a campaign and who received it. Do not target a clinical condition, chart answer, medication, treatment outcome, or minor's care through ordinary marketing audiences without a separate reviewed privacy basis. Beauty & Spa may use nonclinical service/activity segments, still subject to channel consent. This is a proposed edition boundary, not a claim about Boulevard's internal filtering.
- **Evidence — imported status:** Boulevard imports email/text marketing opt-in flags from a previous provider's CSV for existing clients. Its public page describes statuses, but does not establish an audit trail proving the original consent. [Marketing Consent Import](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/9892885-marketing-consent-import). **Proposal:** an import without verifiable source, time and scope stays unsendable for text marketing until fresh consent is collected.

## Minors and clinical privacy

- **Evidence — first-party clinical guidance:** HHS says care-related reminders are allowed for covered providers with reasonable privacy safeguards; voicemail should disclose only what is needed to confirm or ask for a return call. HHS says email may be used with safeguards such as address verification and limiting content, and reasonable requests for alternate/private communication means must be accommodated. This does not mean every medspa is a HIPAA-covered entity or that every SMS provider arrangement is appropriate. [HHS reminder FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/may-health-care-providers-leave-messages/index.html); [HHS email FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/does-hipaa-permit-health-care-providers-to-use-email-to-discuss-health-issues-with-patients/index.html).
- **Evidence — competitor gap:** Boulevard's public material has no documented guardian-specific routing model in the P2A research. Jane, a clinical practice competitor, links related patient accounts with distinct permissions to receive appointment emails and reminders or billing communications; it cautions clinics to consider confidentiality before linking and notifies the patient when a relationship grants new access. [P2A research](boulevard-booking-identity-2026-10-05.md); [Jane Relationships](https://jane.app/guide/client-relationships).
- **Proposal:** For a child, notices go to the verified parent/guardian contacts authorized for that specific communication; the appointment remains on the child's record. Prefer neutral wording (clinic name, time and safe link), never treatment, diagnosis, form answers, photos, medication or chart details in ordinary SMS/email. A guardian's marketing consent is their own choice and is not consent to market using the child's clinical history. When proxy authority changes, the send route changes before the next notice. For confidential minor care permitted by applicable law, use its restricted contact route rather than a blanket parent copy. State-specific review of minor confidentiality and age rules remains required. [Jane Relationships](https://jane.app/guide/client-relationships); [HHS email FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/does-hipaa-permit-health-care-providers-to-use-email-to-discuss-health-issues-with-patients/index.html); [P2B clinical research](boulevard-clinical-records-2026-10-06.md).

## Ratings, public reviews and recovery

- **Evidence — Boulevard:** On eligible plans, post-checkout rating requests can send by email/text after 1 hour to 1 week. Clients give stars and written feedback; staff can filter ratings, get low-rating alerts and send an email reply. Boulevard prompts only clients giving 4–5 stars to share on Google/Yelp/Facebook; clients giving 3 or below do not get that prompt. [Rating requests](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/10207593-rating-requests).
- **Evidence — platform/policy boundary:** Google's Business Profile policy prohibits merchants from discouraging negative reviews or selectively soliciting positive ones and permits neutral requests for genuine experiences without incentives. The FTC's guidance likewise says not to ask only customers expected to be positive, and to check the destination platform's rules. The FTC final review rule covers fake reviews, sentiment-conditioned incentives and some suppression, but should not be summarized as itself a universal ban on every rating-first flow. [Google policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114); [FTC marketer guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/soliciting-paying-online-reviews-guide-marketers); [FTC rule Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers).
- **Proposal:** Offer the same neutral public-review opportunity to all completed-visit clients regardless of internal rating, alongside an optional private feedback/recovery route available to all. Low scores still alert authorized staff for timely service recovery; the public link is never withheld or buried because of score. Never offer a reward tied to rating or wording. Protect clinical privacy in review replies: a public reply should not confirm patient status or treatment details. The latter is a product privacy safeguard requiring final clinic policy review, not a specific Boulevard feature.

## AI boundaries

- **Evidence — Boulevard:** Its beta AI Receptionist handles inbound calls, basic business questions, and sends self-booking/manage links; the client completes the booking, not the AI. It does not answer inbound texts, perform outbound reactivation calls, or currently handle consultation-first booking flows. It can transfer to one configured live number or take a message; summaries/transcripts and outcomes appear in the inbox/call log. Clinical businesses get an emergency-disclaimer setup step. Boulevard states its own AI feature is under its BAA and does not discuss PHI; that statement is about Boulevard's arrangement, not ours. [AI Receptionist FAQ](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/16105159-ai-receptionist-frequently-asked-questions); [AI Receptionist setup](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/16105111-ai-receptionist-setting-up-ai-receptionist).
- **Evidence — other AI:** Boulevard's email template editor labels some content tools as AI-assisted. This is editing assistance, not evidence of autonomous marketing decisions or clinical communication. [Email template editor](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/10088699-email-blasts-edit-a-template).
- **Proposal:** Treat AI as optional later capability pending a separate clinical/privacy/provider review. For Medspa & Clinical Wellness, no AI diagnosis, treatment clearance, medical advice, autonomous clinical booking, or disclosure of chart contents. If a future receptionist cannot complete a request, it must give a human handoff or callback task and preserve a reviewable summary. Beauty & Spa can use the same human handoff rule for booking/policy questions. Do not count Boulevard's beta as an initial-release obligation.

## Choices that still need Jafar

1. **Reminder defaults:** Which initial email/text reminders should be on by default, and should same-day texts be offered at launch? The safe confirmation and consent boundaries above do not depend on this choice.
2. **Waitlist alert placement:** Calendar/front-desk queue alone, or also a staff push/email alert? The agreed behavior is staff review and manual booking; extra channels are a workflow preference.
3. **Marketing release slice:** Should the first release include one-time campaigns, preset rebooking campaigns, both, or only consent collection plus transactional communication? This is release scope, not evidence of vendor capability.
4. **Review release slice:** Whether internal ratings and neutral public-review requests launch together, or after core appointment messaging. The platform-neutral public link rule is recommended whenever public-review prompting exists.
5. **AI release timing:** Keep out of first release unless Jafar explicitly prioritizes it after feasibility, privacy and handoff review.

**Evidence limits:** Public pages can change, especially the AI beta. Boulevard's own marketing-consent and rating flows are documented vendor behavior, not assurance that copying them is acceptable under Twilio or Google policy. HIPAA applicability, state rules for minors, healthcare marketing, emergency handling, recording/transcripts, and vendor agreements require review for the actual clinics and states served before launch.
