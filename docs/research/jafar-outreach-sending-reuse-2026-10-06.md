## Provider and country boundaries

- [Brevo's anti-spam guidance](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/209405205-What-is-the-anti-spam-policy-of-Brevo) expects solicited, consented messages and warns that noncompliant accounts may be restricted. The current platform Brevo identity should remain for applications, setup, alerts, and other expected platform mail; it is not an approved cold-prospect sender.
- [Amazon SES sending review](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ses/latest/dg/faqs-enforcement.html) monitors unsolicited or low-quality mail and may review or pause an account. Existing contractor SES delivery capability does not authorize Uplift cold outreach.
- [Gmail's sender guidance](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en) warns against sending to people who did not sign up, requires authenticated domains, and calls for gradual volume increases and easy opt-out. [Google Workspace's acceptable-use policy](https://workspace.google.com/terms/use_policy/) prohibits unsolicited mass solicitations. A connected mailbox is useful for real replies but is not a bypass around provider rules.
- The [US FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) covers business-to-business commercial email and requires truthful identity, postal address, opt-out, and timely honoring of requests. The [UK ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/) distinguishes corporate subscribers from sole traders, and personal data still has separate requirements. [Canada's CRTC](https://web.crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm) generally requires consent, identity, and unsubscribe for commercial electronic messages. [Australia's ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) requires consent, identity, and unsubscribe; an address found online does not by itself prove consent. These examples show why one "outside Asia" rule cannot automatically approve email to every business.
- [WhatsApp Business policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) requires permission for business-initiated contact. Meta's [Instagram guidance](https://www.facebook.com/help/477434105621119?locale=en_GB) warns against repeated unwanted commercial contact. The first release should create personal tasks and log outcomes for channels without a verified, permitted integration.

## Recommended product boundary

Keep every trade business outside Asia recordable and visible in the same lead journey. Jafar still chooses exactly whom to approve. The app checks a second layer at send time: country/channel rule, usable contact source, sender readiness, suppression, and previous conversation. An unverified legal or provider permission holds the contact for review. A lawful personal contact that lacks an integration may become a task, rather than pretending it was sent automatically. No country is excluded from the lead database merely because its automated sending path is not ready.

For an eventual connected email sequence, use a genuine Uplift sales identity with replies returning to the team, separate from setup/security mail and contractor tenant mail. Confirm its actual mailbox host and provider policy before choosing the integration. Keep each first message complete and relevant. HubSpot's [sequence enrollment](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/sequences/enroll-contacts-in-a-sequence) supports per-contact edits and scheduled sends, and its [unenrollment rules](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/sequences/unenroll-from-sequence) stop on replies, meetings, opt-outs, and bounces. Its subsequent email timing is expressed in days; that supports recommending more breathing room for an unanswered cold email even though Jafar's editor default remains an adjustable five minutes. Five minutes is an edit starting point, not evidence of a good follow-up interval or a promise that every channel will send then.
