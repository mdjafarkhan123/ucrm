# HighLevel email gap review

Research date: 2026-08-15  
Scope: UCRM email decisions through Q80, compared with `contractor-email-service-model.md` and `contractoros-email-reference.md`. SMS is intentionally excluded.

## Conclusion

The approved UCRM model already covers the difficult provider, identity, reply, quota, reputation, suppression, closure, and audit decisions. HighLevel supplies several proven interaction patterns that can now be adopted without more grilling. Its shared-domain fallback, global email DND, universal unsubscribe footer, multiple sending domains, rebilling, and over-limit failure behavior conflict with approved UCRM decisions and should not be copied.

## Adopt proven HighLevel behavior without another product question

1. **Make Conversations the shared source of truth, with personal views rather than separate mailboxes.** Provide `All`, `Mine`, `Unassigned`, unread, starred, and saved views; filter by owner, follower, mention, channel, tags, and date; keep unread counts live; support mark read/unread and star/unstar. Assignment controls ownership, followers provide visibility without ownership, and mentions request attention. This completes the approved Q35 reply-routing decision. [HighLevel Conversations](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000006610-getting-started-with-the-conversations-tab), [conversation filters](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48001222121-overview-of-conversation-filters)

2. **Use role-based conversation access.** Staff need explicit permission to view/manage Conversations and therefore to use its reusable content. Apply UCRM tenant and record authorization on top, with organization administrators able to see the shared inbox and ordinary staff limited by their approved role/assignment model. [HighLevel snippets and permissions](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000003707-conversations-snippets)

3. **Add editable snippets separately from full templates.** Snippets are short, folder-organized responses inserted into a manual conversation and remain editable before send. They should follow the same sender, preview, suppression, allowance, and audit rules already approved. [HighLevel conversation snippets](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000003707-conversations-snippets)

4. **Keep workflow-step email content as a controlled copy, with optional explicit synchronization.** HighLevel copies a selected template into a workflow step, permits local edits, supports test sends, and can explicitly synchronize selected workflow uses. For UCRM, default synchronization off; show where a template is used and require impact review before syncing. This sharpens approved Q61-Q62 without overwriting contractor content. [HighLevel workflow email templates](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000005553-flow-for-email-templates-in-workflows)

5. **Expose automation execution evidence from the message.** Each automated email should link to its workflow execution details, while the workflow exposes per-contact enrollment and step history. Keep message delivery history and automation execution history linked but distinct, as approved in Q68. [HighLevel workflow execution logs](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000003992-workflows-improved-execution-logs-enrollment-history)

6. **Offer manual forwarding of one inbound message as an audited action.** Forwarding is distinct from ownership/reassignment and should not disclose the whole conversation automatically. Require conversation permission, show the external recipient, and record the forward in history. [HighLevel forwarding from Conversations](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000007241-forward-emails-from-conversations-in-highlevel)

7. **Show allowance state as a staged operational panel.** Display current stage, daily/period limit, usage, status, recommendations, and every limit-stage change. Preserve UCRM's approved configurable warm-up values and Jafar overrides; adopt the visibility and history pattern. [HighLevel ramp-up and stage history](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000007790-lc-email-sub-account-rampup-smart-sending-graduation-shared-domain-reputation-protection)

8. **Keep forward-to-assigned-user optional and secondary.** Replies always remain in UCRM Conversations; when enabled, forward a copy to the contact's assigned user's staff email. Charge/count the provider submission consistently and expose it in usage. A copied reply address must not work as a general inbound address. This is the proven completion of Q35, not a replacement for UCRM's opaque reply alias. [HighLevel reply and forward settings](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48001155000-email-services-configuration-reply-forward-settings)

9. **Treat inbound-new-message automation as a later capability distinct from reply automation.** HighLevel separates `Customer Replied` from an inbound-email trigger that can accept a new message from an unknown sender. UCRM's approved first release receives replies to UCRM-sent email only, so record full mailbox/new-lead ingestion as a later Communications campaign branch rather than blending it into reply routing. [HighLevel inbound email trigger](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000007650-workflow-trigger-inbound-email)

## Genuinely UCRM-specific unresolved choices

These are not answered by HighLevel and should remain UCRM decisions rather than assumed competitor parity:

1. **Exact staff visibility boundary:** whether ordinary staff may see all organization conversations or only assigned/followed/mentioned conversations. HighLevel demonstrates both shared views and permission controls, but the correct UCRM boundary depends on contractor roles and sensitive client/work records.
2. **Forwarding and archival limits:** whether external forwarding is available to all conversation users or only administrators, which domains may receive forwards/archives, and whether sensitive quote, invoice, or payment content needs stronger restrictions.
3. **Conversation lifecycle:** when an opaque inbound alias expires, when a conversation is closed/reopened, and how replies to a closed or deleted work item are presented. HighLevel's help material does not establish UCRM's retention and work-object rules.
4. **Operational preference UX:** how clients express optional-reminder preferences before marketing exists, and whether preferences are organization-wide or additionally scoped by reminder category. The approved policy is clear; the customer-facing control model is not.
5. **Allowance economics:** package defaults, reserves, warm-up, rate limits, and Jafar flexibility are approved, but only real Brevo cost/usage data can validate future numeric changes. HighLevel's LC Email limits and rebilling economics are not transferable to a shared Brevo account.
6. **Failure recovery timing:** bounded retry intervals, terminal expiry by message type, and when Jafar intervention is required. HighLevel exposes execution history, but UCRM must define its own Brevo retry and stale-business-state rules.

## Proposed next position

Close the general email grill after resolving only the six UCRM-specific choices above. Carry proven inbox views, snippets, workflow-copy behavior, linked execution history, audited manual forwarding, allowance-stage visibility, and optional assigned-user forwarding directly into the future Communications campaign specification. Keep marketing, full mailbox ingestion, and broad inbound-new-lead email as separately gated campaign work.
