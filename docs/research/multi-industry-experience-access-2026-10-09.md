# P3 primary-source findings: experience composition and access

Researched 2026-10-09. This note distinguishes observed vendor behavior from proposed Uplift decisions. Sources are first-party Boulevard and Stripe materials plus OWASP's published authorization guidance. The [P1 source audit](multi-industry-current-state-audit-2026-10-09.md) supplies local context, not evidence of live readiness.

## Verified facts

1. **Boulevard shares the appointment platform, then layers medspa capabilities.** Its billing guide lists Salon & Spa and Medspa tier families. It says Medspa bundles a platform tier with a Medspa Add-On. The add-on adds photo markup, supervisor review/sign-off and HIPAA-related controls, including a clinical section for allergies, medications and prescriptions. Forms and Charts and ePrescribe have separate plan/add-on treatment. This is evidence for composable capability families, not proof that all medical tasks are covered. [Boulevard bill guide](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/15521146-understanding-your-boulevard-bill); [Medspa Add-On](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/9084775-medspa-add-on).
2. **Clinical data has distinct gates even inside the shared client profile.** Boulevard's permission glossary has separate view/edit/remove grants for allergy and medication notes, and it labels clinical details and medication permissions with Medspa coverage prerequisites. Its forms/charts guide says medication and sex-assigned-at-birth connected fields are for clinical businesses only. These are more specific than a general client-profile permission. [Granular permission groups](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/11662777-granular-permission-groups); [Forms and Charts connected fields](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8258319-forms-and-charts-connected-fields).
3. **A job title and system permissions are different things.** Boulevard says Staff Role determines whether a person performs services, appears for booking and which services they can provide; a permission group controls what they can see and do in the Dashboard and Professional App. Groups can be customized; some permissions have dependencies and some are business-wide or location scoped. [Staff Roles](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941382-staff-roles); [Granular permission groups](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/11662777-granular-permission-groups).
4. **Role templates begin narrow and split duties.** Boulevard describes Front Desk access to scheduling and client management without advanced settings/reporting, Service Provider access centered on own appointments, Accountant access to financial records without clients/scheduling, and Medical Director access to clinical review without finance or user management. It cautions that actual access varies by configuration and assigned locations, and some roles are rolling out by package. [Default roles and permission groups](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/13382959-default-roles-permission-groups).
5. **Reports and mobile surfaces need the same underlying access decision.** Boulevard's report permissions govern tab visibility, categories, own-versus-everyone scope, exports and dashboard widgets; a report widget depends on permission to the underlying report. It also notes some legacy/mobile report limits. The Professional App itself requires a permission. [Reporting Permissions](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/15609508-reporting-permissions); [Professional App](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/9180930-professional-app).
6. **A clinical subscription can change a customer-facing identity step.** Boulevard says its HIPAA-covered portal rollout asks for date of birth after email/text code before showing appointments, forms or profile details. Its Medspa Add-On guide says service details are hidden from patient communications by default. These are examples of stricter surface behavior, not universal regulatory requirements. [Client Portal](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8648439-client-portal); [Medspa Add-On](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/9084775-medspa-add-on).
7. **Commercial entitlements and person permissions are separate concepts.** Stripe describes feature entitlement as customer access arising from pricing, including grant/revoke as subscriptions change. Boulevard separately describes staff permission groups that grant individual actions and scopes. [Stripe Billing entitlements](https://stripe.com/billing/features); [Boulevard permission groups](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/11662777-granular-permission-groups).
8. **Direct requests need their own authorization.** OWASP recommends deny by default, permission checks on every request and object-specific checks. It explicitly warns that client-side controls are not decisive and advises checking the action, object and tenant at the protected service. This supports guarding page data, APIs, exports and file downloads regardless of menu visibility. [OWASP Authorization Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html); [Authorization Patterns](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Patterns_Cheat_Sheet.html).

## Inferences and proposed P3 choices for Uplift

- Resolve access as an intersection: **supported Industry experience and capabilities ∩ compatible, active Package edition ∩ team-member action/record scope ∩ workflow readiness and public release where relevant**. An additional capability may extend a mixed business within its primary experience, but cannot make the clinical safety gate disappear. This is a Uplift design inference from the Boulevard/Stripe separation above and the approved Uplift admission rules.
- Use one shared appointment foundation for Medspa and later Beauty & Spa; put charting, clinical notes, consent/clearance, clinical photos and sensitive patient communications behind a clinical extension with explicit safeguards. Avoid classifying a service as clinical from its display name alone. Boulevard's packaging supports this direction but does not define Uplift's exact boundary.
- Keep **role templates** as convenient starting points; evaluate actual grants and record relationship for each action. A clinician's appointment access does not imply finance access; front desk scheduling does not imply full chart access. The Uplift Control Room remains a separate actor context.
- Generate menu, dashboard, Setup branch and direct endpoint decisions from the same effective capability vocabulary. Hide an unavailable destination for clarity; deny a direct URL/API/export/file request using current server-side facts. If experience/edition/permission or a clinical prerequisite is unknown, deny the protected action and show the responsible person how to resolve it.
- Treat Package compatibility as a publication and assignment rule, not merely a sidebar filter. A Contractor edition must not be assigned to Medspa or expose field-service operations there. A Medspa bundle may contain ordinary appointment services, but clinical records retain the Medspa access/safety posture. Preserve the agreed edition's history when packages evolve.

## Evidence limits

- Public Boulevard documents explain visible behavior, packages and grants; they do not expose its internal architecture or prove its direct-URL/API enforcement. The direct-request rule above comes from OWASP, not a claim about Boulevard's implementation.
- Boulevard's named plans, permissions and rollouts can change. Uplift should define stable internal capability keys and review compatibility when publishing an edition; its current manual offsite billing is not Stripe billing.
- Sources do not determine Uplift's exact feature matrix, clinical legal scope or first-clinic readiness. Those require the approved Medspa contract, state-specific review and a later implementation audit.
