# Boulevard P2E operations research

Checked 2026-10-07. This is evidence for product planning, not a release or build approval. **Confirmed** means Boulevard's public documentation says it. **Specialist pattern** means another first-party product source documents it. **Gap** means the reviewed public sources do not settle the behavior; it does not prove Boulevard lacks it. Jafar agreed the product directions below on 2026-10-07; the settled behavior is in [the plan](../boulevard-product-behavior-contract.md#operations).

## Staff access and daily exceptions

| Finding | Evidence | Planning implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Confirmed:** Boulevard separates a staff member's job role from their permission group. Its default Admin, Location Manager, Front Desk and Service Provider groups are starting points; actual access depends on configuration and location. Its Medical Director and Accountant examples are in gradual, package-limited rollout. | [Staff Roles](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941382-staff-roles); [default groups](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/13382959-default-roles-permission-groups) | Do not assume job title grants clinical or finance access. UCRM role templates can start access, but the effective permissions must govern actions. |
| **Confirmed:** Staff permission groups cover web and Professional App. Managers may create/assign groups only within their own locations and permissions, and cannot raise their own access. Groups contain dependent permissions. | [Granular permission groups](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/11662777-granular-permission-groups); [permission FAQ](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/13383847-permissions-faqs) | Preserve server-side limits on who may delegate access. Single-location first simplifies scope, but the model should not silently give a location manager business-wide authority. |
| **Confirmed:** Staff profile management, assigning permissions/locations, deactivation, scheduling, timecard viewing/editing, catalog changes, inventory changes, document reading/editing, sign-off, reporting and exports are separately permissioned. Boulevard permits bulk clinical sign-off and bypass approval as distinct rights. | [Granular permission groups](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/11662777-granular-permission-groups) | Treat high-impact actions individually. Clinical signing and bypassing review cannot be inferred from generic chart-edit access. |
| **Confirmed:** Boulevard distinguishes log out (end active sessions), suspend (block login while future appointments remain visible for reassignment), and deactivate (block login but hide future appointments on calendar; appointments are not canceled). | [Logging Out, Suspending, and Deactivating Users](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941450-logging-out-suspending-and-deactivating-users) | A departing provider's access should be revoked immediately while future bookings remain in a visible reassignment queue. **Proposed improvement:** never hide uncanceled future bookings because of staff deactivation. |
| **Confirmed:** Boulevard's daily close guide calls for resolving today's appointments, open orders, register cash, and open timecards. | [Daily Opening & Closing Procedures](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/5941317-daily-opening-closing-procedures) | P2E should define a daily unresolved-work queue, including clinical clearance/documentation and stock exceptions where applicable. **Gap:** the article is guidance, not proof of an enforced close workflow. |

## Clinical access, audit and security

| Finding | Evidence | Planning implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Confirmed:** Boulevard distinguishes client personal details, clinical details, allergy and medication notes, document list, document details, document edit, photo gallery, chart sign-off and bypass permissions. Seeing a document list does not itself allow opening it. The Medical Director template is for clinical oversight and excludes user, settings and finance management. | [Granular permission groups](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/11662777-granular-permission-groups); [default groups](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/13382959-default-roles-permission-groups) | Design separate front desk, treating clinician, clinical reviewer and inventory access. A business owner/admin should not automatically read every chart merely by administering staff or billing. |
| **Confirmed:** Boulevard's PHI requirements tell clinics to configure least-privilege groups, logout within 15 minutes or less, keep PHI in intended areas, keep it out of email/text, revoke former staff, disable unsafe shared portal profiles, and conceal service details in notices. | [PHI security requirements](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/8550292-protected-health-information-phi-security-requirements) | Clinical access must be enforced on every route, file, export and integration; safe display and notifications are part of the workflow. This source states Boulevard customer duties, not proof that UCRM is compliant. |
| **Confirmed:** Boulevard audit log access starts with admins and can be delegated by permission. It is business-wide by default, records some action types only since January or May 2026, and one user action can create multiple lower-level events. | [Audit Log FAQ](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/15061025-audit-log-faq) | Do not assume a vendor audit log is complete for all history or meaningful at one row per staff action. Define UCRM audit coverage before clinical use. **Gap:** reviewed Boulevard page does not establish clinical record *read* logging, retention, tamper resistance or emergency access. |
| **Primary regulator evidence:** For entities under the HIPAA Security Rule, HHS requires access controls, unique users, audit controls, authentication and integrity controls; HHS says shared staff IDs do not meet unique identification. HHS's audit protocol asks for policies and evidence of activity review. | [HHS Security Rule summary](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html); [HHS unique user ID FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/does-the-security-rule-permit-a-covered-entity-to-assign-the-same-log-on-id-to-multiple-employees/index.html); [HHS audit protocol](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/audit/protocol/index.html) | **Proposed:** individual staff identities; audit clinical reads, edits, downloads/exports, permission changes and emergency access with who/when/which person/why where relevant; designated review process. Have clinical/legal counsel set exact retention and review requirements. |
| **Primary regulator evidence:** The HIPAA Privacy Rule does not itself set a medical-record retention period; state law generally does. HHS says safeguards apply while retained and during disposal. | [HHS retention FAQ](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/does-hipaa-require-covered-entities-to-keep-medical-records-for-any-period/index.html) | **Gap:** no universal delete-after-N-years or immediate patient-request deletion rule. State-specific retention, amendment and disposal policy remains unresolved. |
| **Confirmed:** Boulevard has two-step verification configuration, nightly web logout and Professional App periodic logout, plus configurable inactivity logout. Its two-step rollout and international phone support had rollout caveats in September 2026. | [Two-step FAQ](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/15636343-updates-to-session-duration-and-two-step-verification-faqs); [Session duration](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/9347071-session-duration-and-automatic-logout) | **Proposed:** require stronger authentication for clinical and privileged staff, short inactivity timeout for PHI surfaces, immediate session revocation on suspension, and renewed verification for sensitive account actions. Do not copy a vendor-specific nightly timer as a clinical guarantee. |

## P2E decision record

**Agreed by Jafar:** Q1, Q3, Q5's emergency access route, and Q6, as recommended. Q4's stock exception controls follow the agreed tracked-stock direction and the recommended safe rule. Jafar's approval sets behavior, not first-release timing. Exact state retention periods, drug rules, and emergency procedure details require later clinical/legal review; no universal values are assumed.

**Safe defaults under the existing direction:** Q2, Q5's individual audit coverage, and Q7 follow the agreed clinical safety and complete-workflow direction. Immediate revocation, preserving visible appointments, accountable audit, and a daily exception list are recorded in the plan.

1. **Access templates:** Should initial templates be owner/operator, front desk, treating clinician, clinical reviewer/medical director, and stock manager, with individual permissions underneath? **Recommend yes.** Keep clinical record read, chart edit, sign-off, clearance, stock adjustment, refund, export and staff-access management separate. The Medical Director title alone must not grant every clinic action.
2. **Departing staff:** Should suspension immediately end sessions while appointments stay visible for reassignment and historical attribution remains? **Recommend yes.** Do not let a hidden booking become an unhandled patient visit.
3. **Stock tiers:** Should UCRM distinguish ordinary retail/professional supplies from clinical lot-tracked products? **Recommend yes.** For injectables, require lot, expiry and visit-level consumption before claiming clinical inventory coverage. If that cannot be delivered in the first release, explicitly exclude those products from managed stock and keep their external system of record.
4. **Stock exceptions:** Who may approve recount differences, write-offs, return-to-stock and emergency use when the recorded count is wrong? **Recommend:** staff can propose a counted variance; a stock manager approves material loss/adjustment with a reason. Returned clinical products stay quarantined unless a qualified person documents they are safe to re-enter stock. Thresholds and drug-specific rules need clinic policy.
5. **Audit and emergency access:** Should clinical reads/downloads and emergency access be recorded and reviewable, including a reason and later review for emergency access? **Recommend yes.** A general change log is insufficient to explain who opened a chart. Define emergency access with clinical/legal review before launch.
6. **Deletion/retention:** Should a client deletion request trigger a reviewed privacy workflow that preserves records while a state retention duty applies, with restricted access and final documented disposal when eligible? **Recommend yes.** Exact US state retention periods and exceptions remain a legal-policy decision, not a product-wide numeric default.
7. **Daily close:** Should the clinic have an unresolved-work list for incomplete visits/orders, unsigned charts, clearance exceptions, open timecards, stock variances and pending recall holds? **Recommend yes,** with owners and due dates. P2F can later define management measures; P2E should settle action ownership.

## Existing app reuse check for parent agent

The [contractor settings blueprint](../contractor-settings-blueprint.md) already defines role templates, individual exceptions, immediate permission changes, staff deactivation, and an account activity view. These are candidates for reuse, but clinical field/document permissions, clinical read auditing, lot-level stock, and retention controls are not established by that blueprint. Existing appointment/stock implementations should be checked separately before any readiness claim.
